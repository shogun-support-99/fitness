Built for what lifters actually ask for.

The most common reason people switch workout apps is logging speed. The most common reason they stay is seeing progress. IronLog is designed around exactly those two.

  1. Log a set in seconds

    Weight and reps arrive prefilled from last session. Tick the set and get back under the bar. “Too many taps mid-set” is the top complaint about workout apps.

  2. Know what to beat

    Last session's numbers sit in every row, so progressive overload is a decision, not a memory test.

  3. A rest timer that runs itself

    Starts when you tick a set, survives reloads and tab switches, and takes a +15s nudge mid-count without losing the sequence.

  4. Charts that prove progress

    Weekly volume, muscle balance, streaks and a per-lift estimated 1RM trend: the chart you open when you think you've plateaued.

  5. Records worth celebrating

    Heaviest weight, most reps and best estimated 1RM are tracked separately per lift, named in the session summary and flagged the moment they fall.

  6. Private by architecture

    No account, no server, no analytics. It is the thing Strava users keep asking for. Everything stays in your browser, exports as one JSON file, and works offline.

Questions, answered plainly.

The short answers, without the marketing.

Is IronLog really free?

Yes, and there is nothing to upsell. There is no account to subscribe to and no premium tier. The whole app is a static site.

Where does my data go?

Into this browser's local storage, on this device. Clearing site data removes it, and it does not sync between devices, so export a backup if your history matters.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit the app is cached, so it opens and logs sets without a connection. Exercise instructions download when you open them.

Which exercises are included?

1,316 MIT-licensed exercises sourced from the public exercises-dataset, each with a gym visual demo and steps across 10 instruction languages. You can add your own.

Can I move my log to another device?

Export from Settings and import the same file anywhere. It is one JSON document containing every session, routine and setting.

How is IronLog different from Strong or Hevy?

The same core loop: fast logging, previous numbers, a rest timer, records and charts, but free with no account and no routine caps, offline-first as an installable web app, and your data never leaves your browser. Trade-offs, honestly: no Apple Watch app yet, and no social feed.

How is it different from Strava?

Strava is a social network for GPS sports: feeds, kudos, segments. IronLog is a private strength log: no feed, no tracking, nothing uploaded anywhere. When you want to share a session, copy the summary as plain text and paste it wherever you like.

Can I import my history from Strong or Hevy?

IronLog imports its own JSON export on any device, so moving browsers or phones is one file. Other apps export CSV, which this version doesn't parse yet. CSV import is on the roadmap; for now, start fresh or keep both.

Does it work on my smartwatch?

Not yet. Watch apps are a real gap we're aware of. The phone app works fully offline, and the rest timer keeps counting when the screen locks.
